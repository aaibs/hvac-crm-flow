Part 1 · The industry

What the job is, and why an email starts it.

A ductwork subcontractor fabricates and installs the sheet-metal air distribution of a building — the galvanised trunks, branches, bends and fittings that carry conditioned air. They don't win work by selling; they win it by pricing. A head contractor sends drawings and a request to bid, and the subcontractor who returns the sharpest defensible number takes the package.

The product

Rectangular, round and oval duct in galvanised steel, sized by gauge; plus fittings, flanges, supports and insulation. Made to SMACNA construction standards and Australian codes.

The trigger

Work arrives as a Request for Quotation — an email from a builder with a tender pack: architectural and mechanical drawings, a scope, and a due date. That email is the start of the pipeline.

The stake

Bid too high and you lose the job; too low and you win a loss. Everything downstream — the take-off, the standards, the versioning — exists to make one number both competitive and correct.

The reference tenant through this deck is Premier Ducts, an Australian subcontractor; the worked example is the QEII Hospital project — about $1.05M priced across 6,636 take-off lines.

Part 1 · The estimate

Anatomy of a take-off — where the money is.

Estimating means measuring the job off the drawings, line by line. Each line is a physical fact — a duct segment or fitting with a size, a gauge and an insulation spec — that resolves into two costs: the material to make it and the labour to fabricate and install it.

ONE TAKE-OFF LINE · the atom of a bid
area m²gaugeinsulationweight kgfitting typeseam / joint

Multiply thousands of these across a project and you get the sheet-metal quantity, the labour hours, and — rolled up — the price. The platform stores exactly these facts per line in takeoffitemsnapshot.

Part 1 · The roll-up

From thousands of lines to one quoted price.

A bid is a hierarchy. Individual take-off lines aggregate into building areas, areas group into commercial packages, and the packages sum to the quote the builder receives. The system carries that structure end-to-end so a single number stays traceable back to the line that produced it.

One deal, many packages

A project is split into packages that are estimated, quoted and won or lost independently — so the system tracks an outcome per package, not per deal.

The maths lives outside

The heavy material-and-labour computation is done in Excel today; the platform captures the take-off geometry and the resulting grandTotal. Why, and what that means, is stage ⑤.

Part 2 · Activity view — front half

Who does what: RFQ email → submitted quote.

Rows are who acts — Builder, Sales, Estimator, the System; diamonds are decisions. A Lead can start two ways: a person logs the RFQ (built today), or the system auto-ingests the inbox (roadmap) — drawn dashed, the deck's mark for not-yet-built.

Part 2 · Activity view — back half

Award → per-package decisions → handover.

This is the fan-out you asked about: a submitted Quote covers several packages (openPackageIds); recording the outcome writes one Decision per package — some WON, some LOST/NO_BID. Each WON decision earns its own Handover Snapshot, and you choose which to release to production first.

Part 2 · ② Qualify

The lead funnel — with escape hatches.

A lead climbs a three-stage funnel, but real sales work needs exits: it can be parked, resumed exactly where it left off, or fast-tracked straight into a deal. Every edge below is a real method on the Lead aggregate.

start · qualify · convert · hold · resume · disqualify · cancel — resume returns to the pre-hold stage (ADR-0006), a quick-deal skips the gate (ADR-0005), and held leads can still be disqualified or cancelled directly. Converting a lead is what mints the Deal.

Part 2 · ③ Deal

A Deal — with real stakeholders and an owner.

Converting the lead opens a Deal: the opportunity you actively work. It carries the people on the other side, an owner and stage SLAs on ours, and a status model that is deliberately richer than a single "open" flag.

"Open" is three states

DealStatus: OPEN · ACTIVE · ON_HOLD · CLOSED · ARCHIVED.

An open deal means OPEN | ACTIVE | ON_HOLD (ADR-0007) — broader than the literal status, and it's what the Companies list counts.

Actions, not field edits

Movement is explicit, audited verbs: ChangeDealStage · Hold · Activate · Close · Archive · Reassign — each an event on the rail, each RBAC-checked.

Stakeholders, per deal

Contacts attach through deal_contacts with a role, and at most one primary (ADR-0013 — a unique partial index; the legacy deals.contact_id is retired).

Part 2 · ④ Tender package

The commercial container: package, versions, documents.

Under a deal, the biddable work is organised into tender packages — the unit that gets estimated, quoted and decided. Each package versions its scope, holds the builder's documents, and is assigned to an estimator to work.

tenderpackage

The package

The biddable slice of the project. Runs its own twelve-state life-cycle (shown at stage ⑦) so packages win or lose independently.

tenderversion

This is the hand-off point between sales and estimating: once a package is assigned and marked READY_FOR_ESTIMATION, the estimator opens a take-off run against it — stage ⑤.

Part 2 · Document governance

Three pointers keep you off the wrong drawing.

The costliest mistake in ductwork is pricing or fabricating against a superseded drawing. CRM v12's revision spine prevents it: every package carries three independent revision pointers, a drawing register holds every issue and addendum, and an addendum's impact is analysed before it can move anything.

Register & links

A drawing register holds every issue; document_package_link (n-n) attaches documents to the packages they belong to. Files live in S3.

Preliminary "P"

A rev marked P enters as basis = PRELIMINARY_TENDER and carries a PRELIMINARY badge downstream until the IFC drawing supersedes it (seam CM-22).

One drift, one door

Only an addendum's impact analysis can raise the PCR that moves a take-off — the same no-drift discipline as the locked run and the frozen handover.

Part 2 · ⑤ Estimate

The take-off run — imported, reviewed, locked.

A take-off run is the unit of estimating work against a package. Item lines are pushed in as audited batches, the run is reviewed, and then locked as the immutable baseline the quote is built on.

Imports are audited batches

Each push is one idempotent batch — PENDING → PROCESSING → SUCCESS / PARTIAL / FAILED — from a CSV upload, the CAMduct add-in, or manual entry (presigned upload, ADR-0009). run_code is server-generated (ADR-0010).

Lock needs a MAJ · ADR-0013

A run can't lock without a current MAJ document attached — the CAMduct-format post-run file. The lock is the frozen estimating baseline; every re-import bounces the run back to IMPORTING.

Pricing is external — by design

The platform stores the take-off geometry (takeoffitemsnapshot) and the quote's total; the labour/material maths runs in Excel. A proven engine prototype (3,229-row Excel parity) exists separately. This is open decision D1.

Part 2 · ⑥ Quote

The Quote — versioned, sent, negotiated.

A locked take-off becomes a Quote. Price is never edited in place: every revision is a new immutable version, so what you sent the builder last Tuesday is always recoverable, and a change request is a first-class object rather than a silent overwrite.

Revise moves to NEGOTIATION

Approving a new version pushes the package to NEGOTIATION — the deck's ⑦ decision fan begins here. The number the builder holds is always a specific, sent version.

Change requests & delivery

quotechangerequest captures the builder's asks against a version; quotedelivery records how and when the quote was issued — both on the audit rail.

The total is captured, not computed

The version stores the grandTotal from the external pricing sheet — consistent with stage ⑤'s external-pricing boundary.

Part 2 · ⑦ Decision

The decision fans out — per package.

Zooming out to the package that has carried the estimate and quote: its full twelve-state machine is where the outcome is recorded. One deal can win some packages and lose others, so the decision — WON · LOST · NO_BID — is taken here, not once for the whole job.

Only WON continues. LOST and NO_BID are terminal for the package; the deal's overall result rolls up from its packages' outcomes. A won package is what earns a handover.

Part 2 · ⑧ Handover

What freezes, and crosses to the MES.

The thread ends where production begins. A WON package produces an immutable Handover Snapshot — a frozen copy of the commercial truth. Only an ACCEPTED snapshot crosses the boundary; the MES (production, from Contract onward) is a separate build, and nothing flows back.

Part 4 · Gates

Two immutable gates to the shopfloor.

Nothing reaches a saw by accident. A drawing must be APPROVED_FOR_CONSTRUCTION (4-eyes, approver ≠ uploader) before a take-off can touch it; and the production take-off must be LOCKED before a work order exists — lock has no unlock, only supersede via a new run.

Then: Work Order

DRAFT→PLANNED→RELEASED→IN_PROGRESS→COMPLETED→CLOSED; any state can go ON_HOLD (quality/material). Released only from a LOCKED run.

No edits, ever

Across the whole MES: never edit — create a new version and supersede; snapshots are immutable; exceptions go through a 4-eyes exception_approval.

Preliminary path

A tender drawing can enter as basis=PRELIMINARY_TENDER and carry a PRELIMINARY badge downstream until the IFC drawing supersedes it via a PCR.

Part 4 · Shopfloor → delivery

From a released work order to a signed POD.

Production runs on the floor behind a blocking quality gate: a batch cannot move to its next operation until the checkpoint PASSES. A fail raises an NCR and a quality_hold, and the pieces loop back to rework.

Part 4 · Make it

On the floor: the batch and the cut.

A released work order spawns fabrication batches, and each batch drives CAM jobs on the cutting machines. Both are event-sourced — you pause and resume, you force-close with a reason, you re-dispatch a failed cut, but you never quietly edit.

Part 4 · Gate 3 in detail

The quality gate, and what a fail sets off.

A blocking checkpoint stops the batch until it PASSES. A fail is not a dead end — it mints an NCR that must be dispositioned, and a quality_hold that can block just one operation or the whole piece until it's released.

Blocking checkpoints default on for FINAL · WELD_INSPECT · PRESSURE_TEST — and are forced on for grease-duct (kitchen-exhaust) routing

Part 4 · Ship it

Out the door: storage, dispatch, delivery.

Finished pieces live in reusable storage units that cycle from empty to delivered and back. A dispatch load allocates them to a truck, and a delivery trip carries them to site — closing with the POD that unlocks install and billing.

Part 4 · Costing & margin

Closing the loop: material, cost, margin.

The production flow isn't finished at delivery — the whole point of the MES is to answer "did this job make money?". Material is received into stock, issued to batches, and rolled up against the budget seeded from the estimate; the gap between quoted and actual is where the estimate learns.

Measured against the estimate

The rollup isn't judged in a vacuum — job_cost_budget is seeded from the CRM est_material_budget (seam CM-19), so actual cost is always read against what was quoted.

Bought-out lines

Not everything is fabricated — package_supply_line (F83) carries item_source = SUPPLY items that skip the shopfloor and go straight to receipt and cost.

The gap that teaches — and a snag

Actual vs quoted_minutes closes the loop (the Handover sheet already shows $31/h actual vs $40/h quoted). Note: batch_cost_rollup FINALIZED needs a CLOSED batch — which fabrication_batch doesn't have (M7).

Part 4 · MES ↔ HRM

The people seam: MES clocks, HRM pays.

HRM runs parallel to the MES, not under it. One rule holds the seam together: hours have exactly one source — the MES shift clock — and HRM draws them, never types them. Statutory payroll (PAYG · STP · super) isn't built; it's pushed to Xero / MYOB / KeyPay through a single adapter.

Pay ≠ cost

MES labour_rate is a cost rate (for margin); HRM base_pay_rate is an award/EBA pay rate. Kept separate — the mistake most systems make.

STP Phase 2

Gross must be disaggregated — OT, allowances and leave reported separately, so the adapter must never lump them back into gross. Super Guarantee is 12% (from 01/07/2025).

Portable LSL & records

Long-service leave via state funds (QLeave · LSC · CoINVEST) with the national reciprocal scheme; Fair Work records kept ≥ 7 years. Casual loading 25%.

Part 4 · Pricing (external)

The number that lives outside.

Pricing is done in Excel and the platform captures the total — but a real bid is not one number. The QEII workbook is a 4-tier roll-up with two cross-axes, a 6-dimensional labour function, and rate books versioned by state and period. This is the weight behind decision D1.

Labour is a 6-D function, not a column

f(category × size_class × insulation × job_type × gauge × handling) — 23 categories × 6 size classes, plus insulation and job-type surcharge matrices. Needs 3 rate-matrix tables.

Area has three meanings

CAMduct area (×1.25) 21,027 m² → true cut area 16,822 m² → est. metal area (× waste % per category). ~30% apart — the model has one.

Rate book & scenario & feedback

price_book(state, period) — QLD/NSW columns, 8 coil periods, quote locks 2025.07. Parallel scenarios (Bare 15% vs Pre-lagged 26%). And the Handover sheet already compares actual $31/h vs quoted $40/h.
